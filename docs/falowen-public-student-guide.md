# Falowen Public Student Guide

## Who this guide is for
This guide is for **new students who have not signed up yet** and want to understand how Falowen works before creating an account.

It explains:
- What Falowen is
- How the app is structured
- What each tab/area does
- How exams and practice work
- What to expect before and after signup

---

## What is Falowen?
Falowen is a language-learning platform focused on structured exam preparation (especially German CEFR levels such as A1, A2, B1, B2, and C1), plus guided AI practice for grammar, writing, speaking, and vocabulary.

At a high level, students use Falowen to:
- Follow a course track (daily or weekly learning flow)
- Submit assignments
- Practice exam skills (reading, listening, speaking, writing)
- Review scores and progress over time
- Interact with class discussion features

---

## App structure at a glance
Inside the student app, navigation is grouped into major tabs:

1. **My Course**
2. **Falowen AI**
3. **Results**
4. **Discussion**
5. **Account**

The **My Course** tab includes sections for:
- Course Book
- Submit
- Exam File
- Attendance

The **Falowen AI** tab includes sections for:
- Grammar
- Writing
- Speech
- Vocabulary

> Note: If you join the French program, some section labels are localized to the French program context (for example AI/grammar/writing/speech/vocab labels adapt by program).

---

## Before signup: what you can expect
Before signup, most students first meet Falowen through landing and signup pages. You can expect to:

- Read an overview of the program and exam-focused learning approach
- Create your account through signup
- Be assigned or select a level/program path (depending on admin setup)
- Start from orientation/day-zero style onboarding content for your track

If your program includes placement, you may take a placement or readiness check to determine the best level.

---

## After signup: student dashboard flow
Once signed in, your normal study workflow is usually:

1. Open **My Course** and follow your course content
2. Complete tasks and submit work in **Submit**
3. Use **Falowen AI** tools for extra practice
4. Review test/exam outcomes in **Results**
5. Engage with peers/class updates in **Discussion**
6. Manage profile and preferences in **Account**

- Attendance tracking for class sessions

## Exam-focused learning experience

### A1 design (Lesen + Hören first)
At A1, students build a beginner base with strong focus on **Lesen (reading)** and **Hören (listening)**, while developing vocabulary and core grammar patterns for everyday communication.

### A2 to C1 design (Grammar + Workbook)
From A2 through C1, the course follows a similar structure: **Grammar** and **Workbook**.

Each workbook has four parts:
- **Teil 1**
- **Teil 2**
- **Teil 3**
- **Teil 4**

### B2 and C1 difference
B2 and C1 are designed as self-learning tracks with AI support. Tutor support is provided through email.


Falowen is organized to support exam success, not only casual language exposure. Students typically work through:

- **Guided course progression** (level-based)
- **Skill-specific practice** (grammar/writing/speaking/vocab)
- **Submission + feedback cycles**
- **Result tracking** for improvement decisions

This means your preparation is both:
- **Structured** (clear path)
- **Adaptive** (extra support via AI and targeted practice)

## Typical first-week student checklist
If you are brand new, this checklist helps:

1. Create your account
2. Confirm your level/program placement
3. Open My Course and complete orientation/day-zero content
4. Submit your first assignment
5. Try one activity in each Falowen AI section
6. Check Results after your first graded activity
7. Visit Discussion for class communication
8. Update Account settings

---

## Tips for getting the best results
- Study consistently (small daily sessions beat cramming)
- Use AI tools right after class lessons to reinforce memory
- Track weak areas in Results and revisit matching practice tabs
- Keep your Exam File organized and updated
- Participate in Discussion to stay aligned with class expectations
